The Real Problem

AI that nobody could explain — including the website.

Zaper.ai had a powerful AI-driven workforce management platform — but a website that failed to communicate it. The construction industry users it was built for found the messaging too technical, the features too abstract, and the call to action too buried.

The challenge wasn’t branding. It was clarity. How do you take genuinely complex AI capabilities — voice recognition, automated reporting, biometric attendance — and turn them into a website that a site supervisor or facilities manager immediately understands and wants to try?

“The challenge was to simplify complex features, cater to diverse users with varying needs, and drive demo sign-ups — without dumbing down what the product actually does.”

Problem definition, stakeholder briefing
3 — Industry sectors researched

2 — Conversion goals designed for

Design Process

Five phases. Research first, visuals last.

I ran the full double-diamond design process — not because it’s standard, but because building a marketing website for a product most users don’t yet understand requires evidence, not assumptions.

Phase 01
Empathise

User research and user interviews with construction, MEP, and facility management professionals to identify real pain points and mental models.

Phase 02
Define

User personas and goal statements. Identified three distinct visitor types — each needing a different message but landing on the same site.

Phase 03
Ideate

Brainstorming, card sorting, and user flow mapping. Explored how to structure the site so each visitor type finds their way to the right CTA.

Phase 04
Design

Paper wireframes → visual design → interactive prototype. Every section validated against the original user research findings before moving forward.

Phase 05
Test

Usability testing on low-fidelity wireframes, survey insights, and iterative improvements before final handoff.

User Research & Insights

What users actually expected from a B2B AI website.

I conducted user interviews, competitive analysis of similar platforms, and usability testing on low-fidelity wireframes. These five expectations consistently emerged across all three industry sectors.

Clear Value Proposition

Users expected a concise, jargon-free explanation of what Zaper AI does in the first 5 seconds of landing. Technical language without context caused immediate drop-off.

Use Cases & Case Studies

Real-world examples of AI applications and their measured impact were highly valued — users couldn’t assess relevance without seeing the product in a context similar to their own.

Interactive Demos

Users strongly preferred interactive product demos or embedded videos over static screenshots. Experiencing the product — even briefly — dramatically increased trust and time on page.

Easy Navigation

Intuitive layout with fast access to features, pricing, and contact. On-site professionals often browsed on mobile between tasks — navigation had to work in 30-second visits.

Responsive Design

Mobile-first access was non-negotiable. Construction site professionals discovered the site on their phones — a desktop-only experience would have lost the majority of the target audience.

Trust & Transparency

Enterprise buyers needed to see data security commitments, company credibility, and team information before they’d consider booking a demo or sharing contact details.

Unique Contribution

The Voice Interaction Demo — turning abstract AI into something you can feel.

The standard B2B SaaS approach is to describe the product. I made users experience it. Right below the hero section, I introduced a live voice interaction demo that let any visitor feel Zaper’s core capability firsthand — no account, no onboarding, no friction.

Design Journey

The decisions that turned a complex product into a clear website.

Each of these pivots came directly from user research or usability testing — not preference, not trend-following.

Decision
Before

Hero Messaging

Feature-led hero: “AI-Powered Attendance, Workforce Management, and Voice Reporting” — a list of capabilities.

“Deep Vertical AI Agent for Construction” — a single positioning statement that speaks to industry identity, not feature inventory. Research showed construction professionals responded to specificity about their industry, not generic AI language. The headline became the clearest differentiator from competitors.

Product Demo Approach

Embedded product screenshots with caption text explaining features — standard B2B SaaS approach.

Interactive voice demo that lets visitors actually experience the core AI capability. Usability testing showed screenshots produced low recall — users couldn’t articulate what the product did even after reading the captions. Live interaction produced immediate understanding and dramatically increased demo sign-up intent.

Navigation Architecture

A full navigation menu with dropdowns for Features, Pricing, Industries, Resources, Company — maximising content access.

Streamlined to five core sections with a persistent “Book Demo” CTA always visible. Card sorting revealed users found deep navigation confusing — they wanted to reach product information in one click, not two. Simplifying the nav increased CTA visibility and reduced bounce from the top of the page.

User Journey Design

One generic page flow for all visitors — same content sequence for everyone.

Goal-driven flows designed for three distinct visitor types: enterprise decision-makers (directed towards demo booking), operations teams (directed toward feature detail), and technical evaluators (directed toward integrations and security). Each arrived at their relevant CTA naturally through content hierarchy, not forced redirection.

Mobile Experience

Desktop-first design adapted for mobile — a standard responsive breakpoint approach.

Mobile-first design from the first wireframe. Research confirmed that construction site professionals discovered and browsed the site primarily on phones — often between tasks with limited time. Every section had to work as a standalone 30-second experience before being extended for desktop.

Information Architecture

Five sections. Each one earns its place in the nav.

Card sorting revealed that users wanted fast access to three things: what the product does, whether the company is trustworthy, and how to try it. The final five-section structure maps directly to those three needs.

01 — Home Page
The full picture, fast

Overview of Zaper.ai’s AI-driven workforce management capabilities. Designed to communicate the core value proposition within the first viewport — before any scrolling.

02 — Safety & Security
The enterprise trust layer

Data protection measures, compliance standards, and transparency about how workforce data is handled. Positioned early — research showed enterprise buyers checked this before any other section.

03 — About Company
The human behind the AI

Vision, mission, and team story. Addresses the credibility gap that all AI-first companies face — buyers need to trust the team, not just the technology.

04 — Blogs
Thought leadership at scale

Industry insights, product updates, and AI workforce articles. Builds organic search authority and gives returning visitors a reason to come back between evaluation stages.

05 — Book Demo
The single conversion goal

A persistent call-to-action throughout the site. Clicking it from anywhere leads to a streamlined demo-booking flow — no forms that ask for information the sales team doesn’t need.

Design Principle
Every section serves either trust or conversion

If a section didn’t build credibility or drive a demo sign-up, it didn’t make the final navigation. This principle cut the initial 8-section structure down to five without losing any critical content.

Challenges I Solved

The hard design problems.

01
Making AI feel real to people who’ve never used it
The problem

Construction industry professionals are pragmatic, not tech-forward. Describing AI capabilities in text produced low comprehension and zero emotional connection to the product.

My solution

The live voice interaction demo — visitors experience the AI summarising their own words in real time. This turned a conceptual claim into a personal proof point in under 30 seconds, producing the highest demo sign-up intent of any element on the page.

02
One website serving three different decision-makers
The problem

Enterprise buyers, operations managers, and technical evaluators all landed on the same homepage — but each needed different information to move forward. Generic messaging served none of them well.

My solution

Designed goal-driven content flows mapped to each visitor type. Content hierarchy — not separate pages — guided each persona toward their relevant CTA naturally. User testing validated that all three types found their path without confusion.

03
Building trust for an AI product in a traditionally offline industry
The problem

Construction is one of the least digitised industries. AI-powered workforce tools face a trust gap that tech-first industries don’t — users are sceptical, not curious.

My solution

Embedded trust signals throughout — not just in a dedicated “Security” section. Social proof, team transparency, compliance language, and industry-specific use cases appeared across every scroll depth, so credibility built progressively rather than being asked for upfront.

04
Driving demo conversions without hard-sell tactics
The problem

B2B SaaS websites often rely on aggressive pop-ups, gated content, and forced form fills. Research showed construction professionals actively avoided these — and closed the tab.

My solution

Made the demo CTA persistent and low-friction — always visible, never intrusive. Removed all gated content barriers before the booking form. Users who reached the Book Demo page had already self-qualified through the content journey, resulting in higher-quality leads with less friction.

Reflections

What this project taught me.

Showing beats telling — especially for AI products

The voice demo generated more comprehension in 30 seconds than any copy I could have written. For complex or unfamiliar technology, the most powerful page element is one that eliminates the need to explain.

Industry specificity is a design decision

“Deep Vertical AI Agent for Construction” outperformed every generic AI headline we tested. Knowing your audience well enough to name their industry in the hero is not a marketing decision — it’s a UX decision that affects every conversion metric downstream.

Less navigation means more conversion

Reducing from 8 sections to 5 felt like losing content. It was actually gaining clarity. The best navigation is the one that makes every decision obvious — not the one that covers every possible option.
