The Problem

A domain, no page, and customers clicking through.

clickfor.in Bazar had already bought their domain and started a WhatsApp-based daily essentials business for local neighbourhoods. But anyone clicking the link landed on nothing — an empty domain was worse than having no link at all.

“

We needed something live before we could share the link with our first customers. Not a full site — just something that shows we’re real and makes them want to message us.

01
No landing presence

Domain was live but empty. Sharing the link looked unprofessional and broke trust.

02
Specific duration

Needed a “coming soon” page for a short window, not a full e-commerce site.

03
WhatsApp-first audience

Customers are local, neighbourhood-based — they order through WhatsApp chat, not a cart.

04
Tone had to be playful

Vibrant, encouraging energy — like a market stall, not a corporate grocery chain.

Design Decisions

Every choice had a reason.

Tone
Market-stall energy over minimal tech

The brief said playful and vibrant. The design pulls from a physical market aesthetic — awning stripes, earth tones, a bouncing dot in the logo — rather than generic SaaS clean.

Palette
Deep forest green + warm orange

Green signals freshness and trust. The orange dot from the clickfor.in logo anchors every headline. Together they read “fresh market” not “tech startup”.

Forest #1B3E1F
Orange #E4762C
WhatsApp #25D366
CTA
WhatsApp as the only conversion

One CTA, one outcome: open a WhatsApp chat. No sign-up form, no email capture, no cart. The audience already lives in WhatsApp — meet them there.

Engagement
An in-page match game to hold attention

Rather than a plain hero, I designed a grocery-item match game beside the headline. It makes people pause, play, and smile — a coming-soon page that feels like something, not nothing.

Type
Unbounded display, Figtree body

Unbounded’s wide, bold caps give headlines the market-crier energy. Figtree is warm and readable for the supporting copy — nothing feels clinical.

The Solution

One page. Two layers. Everything it needed.

A single-page landing experience designed to be live in days, play well on any phone, and turn clicks into WhatsApp chats.

01
Brand Identity

Logo mark, colour palette, typographic system, and a visual language rooted in the local market. The orange dot becomes the recurring motif — in headlines, in CTAs, in the animated logo mark.

02
Coming-Soon Landing Page

Sticky nav with floating WhatsApp button, animated headline with the logo’s bouncing dot, marquee ticker, an in-page match game, about section, and a phone/contact block.

03
WhatsApp-First UX

Every interaction path leads to the same place — a WhatsApp chat. Floating button, hero CTA, contact section all route to the same number. Frictionless for a local audience.

04
Live in One Week

Designed and built as a single HTML file — no framework, no CMS, no dependencies beyond web fonts. Deployed to Vercel in minutes. Light, fast, maintainable.

Screen Walkthrough

The details that make it work.

1
Deep green nav grounds the page

Darker than the hero green — creates a clear top anchor. Logo stays minimal: wordmark + the orange dot.

2
Headline animates letter by letter

Each word slides in from below. The bouncing orange dot in “BAZAR•” mirrors the logo — the brand becomes motion.

3
Orange marquee breaks the page

Infinite scrolling ticker at the section boundary. Keeps the market-stall energy alive between sections.

4
One CTA colour, everywhere

WhatsApp green (#25D366) is the only action colour. Everything else recedes. Eyes always know where to tap.

5
An in-page game, not a placeholder

Match the grocery items. Uses the actual product’s emoji vocabulary — broccoli, tomatoes, milk. Coming-soon pages are usually dead weight; this one’s worth visiting.

6
Matches highlighted in orange

Matched tiles glow in the logo’s orange — reinforcing the brand through play without any extra explanation.

7
Score encourages re-visits

High score is saved in localStorage. Repeat visits feel like returning to a market stall — familiar, a bit competitive.

Challenges

What made this interesting.

01
Brief with no visual reference

No existing brand, no moodboard — just the words “playful, vibrant, encouraging.” Had to define the visual world from scratch.

02
Fast turnaround

Domain was already live and pointing nowhere. The client was actively sharing it with potential customers. Every day without a page was a lost impression.

03
Mobile-first audience

Local neighbourhood customers browse on phones, not desktops. The design had to feel native on a phone screen without a dedicated app.

04
WhatsApp as the only CTA

Unconventional for a landing page — most want email capture or account sign-up. Here, the whole business model is WhatsApp chat. Designing for one exit was a constraint and a clarity.

05
Engagement without overcrowding

The game panel idea came late. Fitting it alongside a large hero headline without the page feeling cluttered required careful proportioning.

06
No-framework constraint

Built as a single HTML file for Vercel deployment. Canvas game, CSS animations, accessible markup — all without reaching for a library.

Reflections

What I’d carry forward.

A small brief with a clear constraint is often cleaner to design than an open-ended one. This one was fun.

What worked

The single CTA direction made every decision easier. One exit: WhatsApp. That clarity pushed the design toward an experience that was actually confident — no hedging, no catch-all.

What I’d push harder on

The game was the most memorable detail. If I revisited this, I’d make the game a bigger hero — let it take up more of the viewport, make the score more visible, surface a leaderboard to encourage sharing.

The right tool for the job

No framework was the right call. Vercel + plain HTML means the client can share the file with a developer in six months and they’ll understand exactly what’s happening.

On coming-soon pages

They don’t have to be a blank waiting room. This one was playful, brand-true, and functional on day one. A coming-soon page is still a first impression — design it like one.
